The first month with the agent is a well-informed guess. It has read your website, looked at what suits your kind of business, and planned accordingly. The second month is better, because by then it has something no plan can give it: results from your own followers.

## What gets measured

For every post it publishes, the agent records reach, saves, comments, shares and clicks. It also records what kind of post it was: the topic, the format, the network, the day and the time.

One post on its own says very little. Thirty posts, sorted by those labels, start to say something.

## From numbers to findings

At the end of the month the agent looks for differences that are large and consistent enough to act on. A finding reads like this:

> Before-and-after carousels earn three times the saves of single images. From 6 carousels and 11 images last month.

The second sentence matters as much as the first. Every finding points at the posts behind it, so you can open them and decide whether you believe it. If a finding rests on two posts, it says so, and the agent treats it as a hint and not a rule.

## What changes because of it

The findings feed the next strategy:

- A topic that did well gets a larger share of next month's posts.
- A format that did badly on one network may be tried on another before it is dropped.
- Publish times move towards the hours when your followers responded.
- What you rejected counts too. Topics you keep turning down fade from the plan.

You see the new strategy before anything is drafted from it, with the reasons for each change. You can overrule any of them.

## What it won't do

It won't chase numbers at the expense of your brand. A post that goes against your tone of voice is not an option, however well it might perform. And it won't change everything at once, because then nobody could tell which change made the difference.

The loop is deliberately slow and steady: a little better each month, with the evidence on the table each time.
