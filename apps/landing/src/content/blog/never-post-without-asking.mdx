Ask a business owner what worries them about handing social media to an AI, and the answer is rarely "it will be boring". It is "it will say something I would never say, in public, under my name".

That worry is reasonable. It is also the reason our agent does not publish anything you have not approved. Not the first post, not the hundredth, not a reply that seems obviously fine.

## Why we don't automate this step

Almost everything else in running a social account is patient, repeatable work. Reading a website to learn how a business talks. Planning a month of topics. Writing captions, sizing images, picking a time to publish, reading the numbers afterwards. An agent can do all of it, and do it every week without getting tired.

Approval is different, because it is not work. It is judgement about your own business, and you know things the agent cannot:

- The supplier you mentioned last month has since let you down.
- That "fun" tone is wrong this week, because of something that happened in your town.
- The class the post advertises sold out this morning.

No amount of reading your website will tell an agent these things. A person who takes ten seconds to look at a post will catch them.

## Making approval take minutes

The risk with approval is that it becomes a chore, and chores get skipped. So we spent most of our design time on making it quick:

1. Posts arrive as a stack of cards, and you swipe. Right approves, left rejects.
2. Every card carries a short note saying why the agent made it, so you judge the idea and not only the picture.
3. Editing is one tap away. Change the words, the image or the time, then approve.
4. Any decision can be undone until the moment the post goes out.

Most people clear a week of posts in the time it takes to drink a coffee.

> If you approve nothing, nothing is published. That is the whole promise, and the product is built so that it cannot be broken by accident.

## Rejections are useful

When you reject a post, the agent does not only drop it. It takes note of what you turned down, and the next month's plan leans away from it. After a few rounds you will find you are rejecting less, not because you have lowered your standards, but because the drafts have moved towards you.
