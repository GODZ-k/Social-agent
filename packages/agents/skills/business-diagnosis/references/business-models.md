# Business models of small businesses

The seven models behind `businessModel.howMoneyIsMade` in `business-diagnosis`, what growth means for each, the signals that identify it on a website, the bottleneck it most often has, and what the questionnaire must supply before a brief can be firm. Pick one; when the business runs two, name the one that carries the revenue and note the other.

## 1. Walk-in

**How money is made.** Many small transactions, mostly from people who live, work or pass within walking distance. Cafés, bakeries, delis, shops, bars, takeaways at the counter, barbers without booking.

**Growth means.** More visits per regular, more regulars, higher ticket per visit. Rarely a wider catchment: a café cannot pull people from across town on Instagram.

**Site signals.** Address and hours prominent, "visit us", a menu, no cart or booking, a map. Google reviews are the main public proof.

**Usual bottleneck.** `repeat` for an established site, `awareness` for a new one, `orderValue` when the room is full.

**Must be supplied.** Slow days and hours, typical ticket, whether any list or loyalty exists, rough daily customer counts.

## 2. Bookings

**How money is made.** Appointments or reservations with a fixed number of slots: chairs, rooms, tables, engineers. Salons, clinics, dentists, physios, studios, restaurants that book, trades with a diary.

**Growth means.** Fill the empty slots first (they are sunk cost), then raise the value per slot (treatments, add-ons, premium times), then add capacity (staff, hours). More bookings when the diary is already full is not growth.

**Site signals.** "Book now", a booking widget or a phone number as the main action, a services list with durations, staff profiles.

**Usual bottleneck.** `trust` for anything on the body or in the home over about $150; `conversion` when booking is phone-only; `orderValue` when full.

**Must be supplied.** Slots per week and how full, the slow days, the value of each service, which services have spare capacity, whether staff will appear on camera.

## 3. Online orders

**How money is made.** A cart, checked out from anywhere the business ships or delivers. E-commerce, food delivery, made-to-order goods.

**Growth means.** More first orders from the right people, higher average order (bundles, thresholds for free shipping), repeat orders on the product's rebuy cycle.

**Site signals.** Product pages with prices, "add to cart", shipping information, a returns page. The catchment is wherever they ship, so awareness content can travel.

**Usual bottleneck.** `awareness` for a young store; `conversion` when the checkout is hard or shipping is a surprise; `repeat` for consumables.

**Must be supplied.** Average order value, margin by product, best sellers, delivery area, whether there is an email list and its size.

## 4. Enquiry-to-quote

**How money is made.** A conversation and a quote before any money changes hands, with few high-value jobs. Trades doing installs, builders, consultants, agencies, photographers, wedding suppliers, B2B services.

**Growth means.** More qualified enquiries (ones the owner can quote and win), a higher win rate from proof, and a higher job value. One extra job a month can be the whole plan.

**Site signals.** "Get a quote", "contact us", a portfolio or case studies, no prices or "from" prices, a form or phone number as the only action.

**Usual bottleneck.** `trust`, almost always; `conversion` when the form or the response time is the break.

**Must be supplied.** Jobs per month the business can take, typical job value, how long a quote takes, who answers enquiries and how fast, consented examples of past work.

## 5. Subscription or membership

**How money is made.** A recurring fee. Gyms, classes, box subscriptions, coaching programmes, software.

**Growth means.** More members who stay, longer; churn is the number that matters. A new member who leaves in month two cost more than they paid.

**Site signals.** Monthly pricing, "join", "membership", a trial or taster offer, a members' area.

**Usual bottleneck.** `trust` before the first commitment; `repeat` (retention) once inside; `conversion` when there is no trial.

**Must be supplied.** Member count and monthly churn, price tiers, the trial or taster if any, capacity (class sizes, floor space).

## 6. Wholesale or B2B

**How money is made.** Fewer customers, larger and repeating orders, bought by someone doing their job. A roaster selling to cafés, a bakery supplying restaurants, a supplier to trade.

**Growth means.** A handful of new accounts a year, each worth a lot; keeping the current ones. Consumer social reach is mostly wasted; LinkedIn and direct outreach do the work (as of 2026-09).

**Site signals.** "Trade enquiries", "wholesale", minimum order quantities, a line sheet, "our stockists".

**Usual bottleneck.** `trust` and `awareness` among the few buyers who matter; the brief should say plainly that most social content will not reach them.

**Must be supplied.** Current account count, typical account value, who the buyer is by job title, whether the owner will do outreach.

## 7. Mixed

Two of the above, both real. A café with a wholesale bean line; a salon selling products online; a restaurant with delivery. Name the channel that carries the revenue as `howMoneyIsMade` and put the other in the same sentence as the side line. Diagnose the main channel; treat the side line as a possible `orderValue` or `repeat` offer, not a second lever. When the questionnaire does not say which carries the revenue, ask ("Roughly what share of revenue is the shop vs online?") and mark `confidence` no higher than `medium`.
