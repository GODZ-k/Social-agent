# Lead magnets and low-risk first purchases

The catalogue behind Step 4 of `offer-and-funnel`. A first purchase is what a stranger buys, or takes for free, before the main sale in a high-consideration business (health, home, money, anything over about $150 or a month's commitment). Each entry says what it is, which business types it fits, what makes it work, and what must be asked before it goes in `priorityOffers`.

A good first purchase has four properties: cheap for the business to fulfil, quick for the customer to say yes to, shows the quality of the work, and leads naturally into the main sale. A discount on the main sale has none of the last three, so it is not a first purchase; it is a price cut.

## Assessments and surveys

| | |
|---|---|
| What | A free or fixed-price look at the customer's situation with a written recommendation: a bathroom survey, a skin consultation, a garden visit, a financial review, a website audit |
| Fits | trades, clinics, consultants, agencies, designers |
| Works when | the price is stated (free, or a fee credited against the job), the time is short (30 to 60 minutes), the customer leaves with something written |
| Ask | "What would you charge, and is it credited against the main job?" "How many a week can you do without hurting paid work?" |
| Do not | make it a sales pitch with no written output; offer it free when the owner's time is the constraint (a fixed fee filters tyre-kickers) |

## Small paid service that shows the work

| | |
|---|---|
| What | A low-value job that proves the quality: a boiler service before a bathroom, a hygiene visit before whitening, a blow-dry before colour, a one-hour session before a package |
| Fits | trades, dental, salons, physios, coaches, tutors |
| Works when | it is a service the customer needs anyway and the business already does it; the price is on the site |
| Ask | "Which small service do most of your big-job customers buy first?" "Is there margin in it, or is it a loss leader you accept?" |
| Do not | invent a small service the business does not offer |

## Guides that answer the buying question

| | |
|---|---|
| What | A short written or video guide answering the question people ask before they buy: "What does a bathroom cost in [town]?", "Is Invisalign right for me?", "How to choose a wedding photographer" |
| Fits | any considered purchase; strongest where competitors hide prices |
| Works when | it says real numbers or a real decision rule, and the business is willing to be that plain; it is offered in exchange for an email or a DM only when the owner will follow up |
| Ask | "Are you willing to publish price ranges?" "Who follows up with people who ask for the guide, and when?" |
| Do not | gate it behind a form if nobody follows up; write it as a brochure |

## Taster sessions and trials

| | |
|---|---|
| What | One free or cheap session of the thing: a class, a gym week, a coaching call, a tasting, a sample box |
| Fits | memberships, studios, subscriptions, coaching, food producers selling boxes |
| Works when | the session is the real thing, not a demo, and the follow-up to join is immediate and stated |
| Ask | "How many taster places a week?" "What is the offer at the end of the taster?" |
| Do not | run a taster that is worse than the paid thing; offer it when the class is already full |

## Proof pieces that need no purchase

| | |
|---|---|
| What | Not a purchase but a first step: a consented before-and-after, a walk through the process, a named client's own words, a price list published where competitors hide theirs |
| Fits | anywhere `trust` is the bottleneck |
| Works when | the proof is specific and permitted; publishing prices is itself a first-purchase substitute because it removes the reason to enquire elsewhere |
| Ask | "Which cases can be shown with consent?" "Can prices be published, at least as 'from'?" |
| Do not | use stock imagery or unnamed testimonials; state a result the business cannot repeat |

## Choosing between them

- `trust` bottleneck: a proof piece plus one assessment or small service.
- `conversion` bottleneck: publish prices, then the smallest bookable thing.
- Owner's time is the cap: a fixed-fee assessment, never a free one.
- Membership or subscription: a taster with a stated join offer.
- Retail, café, restaurant: none of these; the first purchase is the product, and the "magnet" is this week's reason to come in.
