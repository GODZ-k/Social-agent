# Seasonal and local calendars

The calendar behind Step 5 of `offer-and-funnel`. Use it to find moments an existing offer fits, never to invent an offer. Dates assume the northern hemisphere and a Western retail calendar; check the country's own holidays with one `web-search` when the brand's address says otherwise (Diwali, Ramadan and Eid, Lunar New Year and the southern-hemisphere seasons move or invert most of this). Compiled 2026-09; dates that move each year (Easter, Ramadan, Mother's Day by country) must be looked up for the year in question.

## Lead times

| Offer type | Post from | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Walk-in impulse (a seasonal drink, a bake) | 1 to 2 weeks before, then through the window | people act on the day |
| Anything booked (tables, appointments, installs) | 4 to 6 weeks before | the diary fills before the moment |
| Gifts and orders shipped | 5 to 6 weeks before the occasion, hard stop at the last shipping date | delivery time and the last-order date |
| Events the business runs | 3 to 4 weeks before, with a reminder in the last week | enough to plan, close enough to remember |
| Weekly slow-period offers | every week, same day, same format | the rhythm is the offer |

## The general year

| Month | Moments | Business types most affected |
|---|---|---|
| January | new-year resolutions, "new year new you", dry January, quiet trade | gyms, clinics, coaches, salons; cafés and restaurants are slow |
| February | Valentine's Day (14), half-term in some countries, Pancake Day (moves) | restaurants, bakeries, florists, gift retail, beauty |
| March | Mother's Day (moves by country: UK in March, US and most others in May), spring cleaning, tax year-end in some countries | florists, bakeries, home services, accountants |
| April | Easter (moves), spring, garden season starts, school holidays | bakeries, garden trades, home improvement, family venues |
| May | Mother's Day (US and many others), bank holidays, wedding season starts, exam season | restaurants, florists, photographers, tutors, beauty |
| June | Father's Day (third Sunday in most countries), weddings, graduations, the summer quiet for indoor trades | restaurants, barbers, gift retail, home trades (quiet: a capacity moment) |
| July | school holidays begin, holidays, outdoor everything | cafés with outdoor space, family venues, ice cream, tourism-facing shops; offices empty, so lunch trade dips |
| August | peak holidays, back-to-school at the end | family venues, retail (back-to-school), B2B is quiet |
| September | back to routine, "September reset", heating checks before winter, wedding season second peak | gyms, classes, plumbers and heating engineers, tutors, cafés near offices |
| October | Halloween (31), half-term, clocks change, cosy season | bakeries, cafés, family venues, home comfort trades |
| November | Black Friday (last Friday), gift buying starts, Christmas bookings open, Diwali some years | retail, online shops, restaurants (party bookings), salons (party season starts) |
| December | Christmas and New Year's Eve, party season, last shipping dates, gift cards | restaurants, retail, salons, bakeries; trades and B2B stop mid-month |

## Notes by business type

**Cafés and bakeries.** Seasonal drinks and bakes are cheap to create and travel well on short video; four to six rotations a year is enough. The weekly slow period (from `capacity`) matters more than any holiday. Gift cards and retail (beans, boxes) in the six weeks before Christmas. January is slow for everyone; a January offer for regulars is a repeat lever, not a seasonal one.

**Restaurants.** Valentine's, Mother's Day and Christmas parties are booked weeks ahead; those posts run 4 to 6 weeks out. Midweek set menus are a weekly seasonal offer. The summer quiet for indoor rooms is a capacity moment. New menus are moments in themselves, two to four a year.

**Salons and barbers.** Party season (November to December) and wedding season (May to September) are bookings moments; the post runs 4 to 6 weeks out. January and the summer holidays are slow: midweek offers for existing clients. Back-to-school at the end of August is a barbers' peak.

**Clinics (dental, skin, physio).** "New year" (January) and "before summer" (April to May) are the two cosmetic peaks; the post runs 6 weeks out because treatment takes time. Winter for skin; spring for sports injuries. Check-ups have no season, so they are a repeat offer.

**Trades.** Heating engineers: September to October for servicing, then emergency work in the first cold snap that social cannot chase. Bathrooms and kitchens: January to March planning, installs in the summer quiet (the owner's `capacity` will say). Gardens: March to June. Roofers and gutters: autumn. Electricians: no strong season; the slow month is the moment.

**Online shops.** The year is Black Friday to Christmas, with last-shipping dates as hard stops, then Valentine's, Mother's Day and Father's Day for gift categories. Consumables run on the rebuy cycle, not the calendar. January is returns and sales.

**Consultants and B2B.** Budget cycles (financial year-end, quarter starts), September and January restarts, the industry's own conference season. August and mid-December to early January are dead.

## Local moments

Find them with one search for "[town] events [year]" or "[town] what's on [month]". The useful ones: markets, festivals, sports fixtures, university terms (a student town empties in June and fills in September), a big employer's shift patterns, road works (a capacity moment nobody plans for), the local team's home games. Write the moment into the offer's `why` with its date, and the source URL into the brief's sources.
